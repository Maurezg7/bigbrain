## Qué son

Los **modales** son verbos auxiliares que agregan un matiz al verbo principal: capacidad, permiso, obligación, probabilidad.

Reglas comunes a todos:

1. Les sigue el verbo en **forma base**, sin *to*: *She can swim.*
2. **No cambian** con la persona: *he can*, no ~~he cans~~.
3. Hacen la negación y la pregunta **sin *do***: *Can you…? I cannot.*
4. No tienen infinitivo ni gerundio: no existe ~~to can~~.

## Resumen

| Modal | Usos principales | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| can | capacidad, permiso informal, posibilidad | I can drive. |
| could | capacidad pasada, pedido cortés, posibilidad | Could you help me? |
| may | permiso formal, posibilidad | May I come in? |
| might | posibilidad menor | It might rain. |
| must | obligación fuerte, deducción segura | You must stop. |
| have to | obligación externa | I have to work tomorrow. |
| should | consejo, expectativa | You should rest. |
| will | futuro, decisión, promesa | I'll call you. |
| would | condicional, cortesía, hábito pasado | I would like a coffee. |
| shall | ofrecimiento, sugerencia | Shall we start? |

## Capacidad

- Presente: *I **can** speak three languages.*
- Pasado general: *I **could** swim when I was five.*
- Un logro puntual en el pasado: *I **was able to** finish on time* (no *could*).
- Otros tiempos usan *be able to*: *I **will be able to** help. I **haven't been able to** sleep.*

## Permiso y pedidos

De menos a más formal:

| Pedido | Tono |
|---|---|
| Can you open the window? | informal |
| Could you open the window? | cortés |
| Would you mind opening the window? | muy cortés |
| May I use your phone? | formal |

## Obligación

| Forma | Significado | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| must | obligación que siente quien habla | I must study more. |
| have to | obligación que viene de afuera | I have to wear a uniform. |
| mustn't | **prohibición** | You mustn't smoke here. |
| don't have to | **no es necesario** | You don't have to come. |
| needn't | no es necesario | You needn't worry. |

> [!WARNING]
> *Mustn't* y *don't have to* **no significan lo mismo**. *You mustn't go* = está prohibido ir. *You don't have to go* = no hace falta que vayas, pero podés.

*Must* no tiene pasado: se usa *had to*. *Yesterday I **had to** work late.*

## Consejo

- *You **should** see a doctor.*
- *You **shouldn't** eat so much sugar.*
- *You **ought to** apologise* (igual que *should*, más formal).
- *You **had better** leave now* (advertencia: si no, habrá consecuencias).

## Probabilidad y deducción

El modal indica cuán seguro está quien habla.

| Certeza | Modal | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| casi seguro que sí | must | She must be tired: she worked all night. |
| probable | should | The package should arrive today. |
| posible | may, might, could | He might be at home. |
| casi seguro que no | can't | That can't be true. |

Lo opuesto de *must* en deducción es *can't*, no *mustn't*.

## Modales en pasado

Se forman con **modal + have + participio**.

| Estructura | Significado | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| must have | deducción segura sobre el pasado | He must have forgotten. |
| can't have | imposibilidad | She can't have seen us. |
| might have, may have | posibilidad | They might have missed the bus. |
| should have | reproche o arrepentimiento: no se hizo | I should have studied more. |
| shouldn't have | se hizo y estuvo mal | You shouldn't have said that. |
| could have | fue posible y no pasó | We could have won. |
| would have | resultado de una condición irreal | I would have helped you. |

En el habla, *have* se reduce: *should've*, *could've*, *would've*. Nunca se escribe ~~should of~~.

## Would

- **Condicional**: *I **would** travel more if I had time.*
- **Cortesía**: *I**'d** like a coffee. **Would** you like some tea?*
- **Preferencia**: *I**'d** rather stay at home.*
- **Hábito pasado**: *When I was a child, we **would** play outside every day.*
- **Futuro visto desde el pasado**: *He said he **would** come.*

## Used to

Expresa hábitos o estados pasados que ya no ocurren:

- *I **used to** smoke.* (antes fumaba)
- *I **didn't use to** like coffee.*
- ***Did** you **use to** live here?*

No hay que confundirlo con *be used to + -ing* (estar acostumbrado): *I**'m used to getting** up early.*

## Errores frecuentes

| Incorrecto | Correcto |
|---|---|
| ~~I can to swim.~~ | I can swim. |
| ~~She cans drive.~~ | She can drive. |
| ~~Do you can help?~~ | Can you help? |
| ~~I must to go.~~ | I must go. |
| ~~I will can come.~~ | I will be able to come. |
| ~~You don't must smoke.~~ | You mustn't smoke. |
